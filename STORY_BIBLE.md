# THE KILOWATT SAGA — Story & Design Bible

## Starring GRAPNEL · v1.2 (director's rulings locked)

> *"The city sold its own heartbeat. Someone has to steal it back."*

This is the canonical source for GRAPNEL's narrative and systems design.
Everything in the game either exists in this document or doesn't ship.
Sections marked **[BUILT]** exist in the current build; **[RETROFIT]** exists
but gets re-skinned by this bible; **[NEW]** is to be implemented.

# PART I — PREMISE & THEMES

## The premise

**Meridian City** runs on a privatized power grid called **the Spine**. Three
years ago the city quietly sold it. Tonight, the man who bought it switched it
off — not to break the city, but to teach it who owns its pulse.

**Ari Vance** was a lineworker on the Spine: third-generation, tower-climber,
the person who kept other people's lights on. When the sale went through, the
whole line crew was fired by an email with no subject line. Ari's father — who
gave forty years and one leg to those towers — died six months later, and the
obituary called him "a retired utility contractor."

So when the blackout came, Ari didn't wait for permission. They took their
father's line-rig — the insulated suit, the throw-anchor, the spool of
graphene cable — and went UP. The press will eventually call the figure
swinging between the dead towers **GRAPNEL**. Ari just calls it *finishing
dad's shift.*

## Why this hero works (the Marvel/DC DNA, made ours)

| Pattern | The comics do it | GRAPNEL does it |
|---|---|---|
| The wound | Batman's parents; Peter's uncle | A father erased by a layoff email — grief tangled with CLASS anger |
| Powers from identity | Spider-Man's powers mirror his awkwardness | Ari's "powers" are literally their JOB: climbing, rigging, reading current |
| The city as character | Gotham's gargoyles, NYC's canyons | Meridian's districts each have an economy, a grudge, and a skyline |
| The mirror villain | Joker to Batman, Vulture to Peter | KILOWATT is what Ari becomes if grief curdles into ownership |
| Great power / responsibility | Uncle Ben's law | "You don't own the line. You HOLD it, for whoever's next." — Ari's father |

## Themes

1. **Infrastructure is love made boring.** Every unglamorous system — power,
   water, transit — is somebody's life's work. The game makes you FEEL a grid.
2. **Grief vs. grievance.** Ari and Kilowatt suffered the same betrayal. One
   holds the line; the other decides the line owes him. Every act contrasts them.
3. **A city is a promise between strangers.** Co-op embodies the theme:
   the mission literally cannot be completed alone at the highest difficulty.

## Tone

Daylight noir. Bright skies, long shadows. Banter on the comms, weight in the
quiet scenes. PG-13: real danger, no gore; villains are wrong, not monstrous —
except where the tragedy is that they COULD have been right.

# PART II — THE WORLD BIBLE

## Meridian City districts **[BUILT — named rings]**

| District | Skyline | Economy | Story role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kilowatt Plaza** (core) | 60–110m glass towers | Spine HQ, finance | The scene of the crime — the sale was signed here |
| **The Exchange** | High-rise | Law firms, city hall annex | Act 3's paper trail lives here |
| **Midtown Rows** | Mid-rise brick | Shops, walk-ups | Ari's neighborhood; where the city feels human |
| **Greenline** | Low + parks | Markets, schools | What's at stake — the district that dies first in a blackout |
| **The Fringe** | Low industrial | Depots, substations | Kilowatt's staging ground; Act 3 battleground |

## The Spine

The grid's arterial trunk runs UNDER the city, with surface "vertebrae" —
substations, junction towers, the antenna forests on rooftops **[BUILT: the
towers you grapple]**. Lore rule: *anything Ari can attach to is part of the
Spine.* The grapple isn't magic — it's a lineworker's throw-anchor finding
live infrastructure. This is why the reticle "recognizes" surfaces.

## The blackout **[BUILT — Act 1 backdrop]**

Kilowatt's opening move: a surgical shutdown sparing hospitals (he's not a
butcher — important) but killing everything else. The eight **signal beacons**
are emergency relays from the PUBLIC grid era — old tech he forgot, because he
only ever valued what he could own. The past defeats him; that's thematic.

# PART III — CHARACTER BIBLE

## ARI VANCE / GRAPNEL — the player

- **Age 26. Lineworker, 3rd generation. Laid off 3 years.**
- Working odd rigging jobs since; lives above a repair shop in Midtown Rows
  with too many tools and one photo of a father on a tower.
- **Voice:** dry, technical, allergic to speeches. Jokes under pressure the
  way linemen do — gallows humor with a safety margin. Never quips at
  civilians' expense.
- **The arc (saga-long):** *from "finishing dad's shift" (private grief) to
  "holding the line" (public promise).* Act 1: rage looking for a task.
  Act 2: discovers being SEEN by the city (fear of the mantle). Act 3: nearly
  becomes Kilowatt when the betrayal goes political. Act 4: chooses the
  promise over the grievance — and says so, out loud, to Kilowatt, who is the
  only person alive who fully understands.
- **Costume:** father's insulated line-suit, re-rigged. Suit tiers (Part VI)
  visibly evolve it from "borrowed workwear" to "mantle."

## The supporting cast

**WREN OKAFOR, 19 — the voice on the line. [NEW — comms system]**
Pirate-radio operator broadcasting from a Fringe rooftop; keeps the old
public-grid emergency channels alive as a hobby that becomes a calling. Wren
is the mission-giver voice, the banter engine, and the audience surrogate.
Arc: from "this is the coolest thing ever" to understanding people can die on
her instructions — the operator's version of responsibility.

**DIDI PALLAV, 61 — the mentor.**
Retired tower-boss; trained Ari's father. Runs the repair shop under Ari's
flat. Knows exactly what Ari is doing by night, says nothing for a full act,
then hands Ari a better anchor spool: *"Your father rigged this. I held it
back until you stopped swinging angry."* Mechanically: Didi gates suit tiers.
Arc: forgiving herself for signing early retirement instead of striking.

**NOA VANCE, 31 — the sister.**
ER nurse. The hospital exemption is the thread she pulls: SHE noticed the
blackout spared hospitals, and tells Ari — making her the saga's first
detective. Doesn't know Ari is Grapnel (Act 1–2); the reveal scene (M11) is
the emotional centerpiece of Act 3. She doesn't say "be careful." She says:
*"Dad didn't fall. The line failed him. CHECK YOUR LINE."*

**LT. IMARA COLE, 44 — the badge.**
Runs the understaffed Grid Crimes unit nobody funded. Wants Grapnel arrested
in Act 1, uses Grapnel by Act 2, and by Act 4 orders her unit to "fail to
apprehend" — Meridian's version of the Gordon compact.

**Fellow Linewalkers [BUILT — co-op players].**
Canon: other laid-off Spine workers who saw someone swinging and got their own
rigs out of storage. Co-op is diegetic: the crew reforms itself. Player 2–4
ARE the story of the city refusing to stay individual.

## The rogues gallery

**KILOWATT / Elias Marrow, 58 — the mirror. [BUILT as voice; NEW as presence]**
Chief engineer of the Spine for 30 years; designed half of it. When the city
privatized, they made him sell it to the buyer — himself, through shells
(the paper trail of Act 3). The city that never thanked him will now PAY him,
literally, forever. He doesn't want to destroy Meridian; he wants it
*metered*. Every drone, every act of control is him "maintaining HIS grid."
- **Voice:** courteous, precise, exhausted. Never shouts. Calls Ari
  "lineworker" with genuine respect — recruitment is his real goal until M14.
- **The tragedy:** he was RIGHT about the betrayal. Ari must beat the man
  while conceding the grievance. The final dialogue does both.

**BROWNOUT / Marcus Okoye-Marrow, 32 — the fist. [NEW — Act 3 boss]**
Kilowatt's estranged son-in-law; ex-substation foreman; huge, deliberate,
carries a induction-coil breaker hammer that kills power (and light) in a
radius around him — fights in a moving circle of darkness at noon. Not
cruel: convinced Kilowatt will restore his family's standing. Arc: the first
lieutenant to realize Kilowatt values the grid over people — defects in M15.

**STATIC / Ife Adeyemi, 27 — the ghost. [NEW — Act 2–3 recurring]**
Signal-jammer and infiltrator; jams Wren mid-mission (mechanic: comms and
HUD degrade), speaks only in samples of OTHER people's recorded voices.
Was the Spine's forensic auditor — she found the forged signature first,
sold it to Kilowatt instead of the press. Pure grievance, no grief. Escape
artist: three encounters, three escapes, captured only by prediction (M13).

**THE AMPS [BUILT as drones; NEW as gang].**
Kilowatt's maintenance corps: part drone swarm, part uniformed crew of
poached utility workers. Human Amps enable hand-to-hand combat (Part V).
Rank-and-file are recruited with REAL paychecks — punching them is
complicated, and the game says so.

**DEPUTY MAYOR CORREN HALE, 51 — the pen. [NEW — Act 3 reveal]**
The signature on the sale is the mayor's — forged by Hale, who brokered the
privatization for a board seat. The saga's true villain in the political
register: Kilowatt at least loved the grid. Hale never once looked at it.
Endgame: exposed by the shard evidence chain **[BUILT: 36 shards]**.

# PART IV — THE CAMPAIGN

### Structure: 4 acts, 16 missions. Act 1–2 retrofit what's built; 3–4 are new.

Notation: **[G]** = gameplay note tying the beat to a system.

## ACT 1 — BLACKOUT PROTOCOL **[BUILT, retrofit]**

**M01 — DEAD AIR.** Cold open: blackout hits mid-shift at Noa's hospital
(backup kicks in — she clocks the exemption). Ari takes the rig out.
Tutorial swing to the plaza. **[G: existing tutorial beacon]**
Wren cuts onto the old emergency channel: *"Whoever you are — you're
standing on the only frequency he doesn't own."*

**M02–M04 — THE RELAY RUN.** Beacons 2–8 across the districts **[G: built]**,
interleaved with Wren decoding the sabotage (existing beacon toasts become
Wren dialogue). Kilowatt's first broadcast at beacon 5: courteous, chilling:
*"Please stop repairing my property, lineworker. I offer wages."*

**M05 — THE GRID BREATHES (Act finale). [BUILT: finale beam]** All beacons
lit; the city roars back; Ari stands on the plaza beam. Kilowatt, softly:
*"Now I know what you love. That was unwise to show me."* → Drones deploy.

## ACT 2 — EYES OF THE OWNER **[BUILT, retrofit]**

**M06 — WITNESS.** Drone patrols begin **[G: built patrols/shoves]**. Cole's
unit tries to arrest Grapnel; Ari refuses to fight cops — traversal-only
escape mission **[G: no-combat constraint]**. Cole, watching the restraint,
starts her turn.

**M07 — THE HUNT. [BUILT: takedowns]** Wren maps patrol lanes; Ari learns
drones die at speed **[G: 20 m/s takedown]**. Static's first jamming — mid
mission the HUD glitches, Wren's voice shreds into samples. First fight
against human Amps (combat tutorial, Part V) **[G: NEW combat]**.

**M08 — PAY DAY.** Follow an Amp payroll van (traffic system **[G: built]**)
to a Fringe depot: Kilowatt PAYS WELL. Optional: eavesdrop crews talking
about feeding kids. Choice beat (flavor, not branch): leave the payroll or
torch it — Wren argues both sides. Canon: leave it.

**M09 — SHARD CHAIN. [BUILT: 36 shards + milestones]** The shard lore
(spine sale, forged signature) formalizes into an evidence board in the
safehouse **[NEW hub]**. Act ends: Noa recognizes Ari's silhouette on a
news clip. Doesn't call. The silence IS the cliffhanger.

## ACT 3 — ROLLING THUNDER **[NEW]**

**M10 — BROWNOUT.** Substations start dying in a moving wave — Brownout's
hammer walks district to district. First boss encounter: unwinnable in his
dark radius; survive and TAG his route **[G: chase + no-win boss teaches his
mechanic]**.

**M11 — CHECK YOUR LINE.** Noa confronts Ari (the scene the saga is built
around). No combat. Safehouse, one room, two chairs. She hands back the
photo of their father: *"He didn't fall. The line failed him. Check your
line."* Unlocks: Noa joins the network as medical support **[G: respawn
buff]**.

**M12 — THE PAPER SPINE.** Exchange-district infiltration: shard chain
points at city hall's records annex. Static ambush — jammed HUD, silent
Wren, dark stairwells: the horror mission. Recovered file: the sale
signature page. Forged.

**M13 — PREDICTABLE.** Trap for Static, sprung by prediction: she always
escapes via the tallest jammer mast **[G: player must pre-rig the mast —
first PLAYER-authored trap]**. Captured, she trades: Hale's name for
protection FROM Kilowatt. *"He meters everything. Including loyalty."*

**M14 — WAGES.** Kilowatt's recruitment scene — rooftop, two coffees, no
fight, full villain philosophy: *"You hold a line someone else owns,
lineworker. I OWN a line someone else holds. One of us is honest."* The
player answers via dialogue choice (all refusals; tone varies). Refusal →
Brownout unleashed on Midtown Rows.

**M15 — THE DARK CIRCLE (Act finale).** Brownout boss, winnable: co-op-
optimal fight — someone kites the dark radius, someone strikes from light
**[G: boss = combat + traversal exam]**. Beaten, he sees Kilowatt reroute
power AROUND a trapped crew rather than lose margin — and stands down.
Defection. Gives up the Surge.

## ACT 4 — THE SURGE **[NEW — finale]**

**M16 — HOLDING THE LINE.** Kilowatt's endgame: dump the Spine's stored
months of power in one surge — not to kill, to RESET: burn every legacy
public relay so only HIS metered grid remains. City-wide finale: every
system in the game fires at once — beacons must be re-lit under drone
assault while the surge front advances district by district (visible as
whitening skies **[G: escalating weather/light state]**), Amps defect
mid-fight after Brownout broadcasts, Hale is exposed on Wren's channel,
Cole's unit "fails to apprehend."
Final confrontation on the tallest core tower, storm of arcing power.
Combat phases interleaved with the saga's LAST dialogue — Kilowatt, beaten,
holding the master breaker:

> **KILOWATT:** They will sell it again, lineworker. Next year. Quietly.
> **GRAPNEL:** Probably.
> **KILOWATT:** Then why.
> **GRAPNEL:** Because you don't own the line. You hold it. *(beat)* For
> whoever's next. Dad's rule. You'd have liked him. You basically WERE him.
> **KILOWATT:** …I metered him. Tower 40. I remember every name I metered.
> **GRAPNEL:** I know. That's the difference. You kept a ledger. He kept
> people's lights on.

Ari throws the breaker: full public restore. Epilogue beats: Didi's shop
becomes the Linewalkers' hall; Cole gets funding; Hale's trial; Kilowatt in
custody, correcting the prison's wiring diagram — *"tell them tower 40's
grounding is still wrong."* Sequel hook: someone ELSE answers Wren's
frequency: *"…is this the line? We have a problem in Harbor South."*

# PART V — COMBAT DESIGN **[NEW]**

Philosophy: **momentum is the weapon.** GRAPNEL's combat must feel like the
swing continued indoors — never a separate stop-and-fight mode.

## Player moveset (unlock order in Part VI)

| Move | Input | Design |
|---|---|---|
| Line Strike | attack in motion | Core hit; damage scales with entry speed (the swing IS the combo starter) |
| Flurry | attack grounded | Fast 3-hit chain; safe, low damage — the "wrong" habit the game trains you out of |
| Yank | grapple an enemy | Pull them to you (light) or you to them (heavy) — crowd rearrangement |
| Slip | dodge | I-frame step; slipping THROUGH a strike charges the next Line Strike |
| Counter-loop | parry window | Rope-wrap counter: reverses a grabbed strike; the "skill read" of the system |
| Spike | air attack downward | Landing hit with AoE — converts height into control |
| Live Wire (T3) | charged | Suit discharges stored swing-energy: radial stun. Earned, spectacular, rare |

**The speed rule:** damage/stagger scale with velocity at contact. Standing
still is the WORST place to fight — the system enforces the fantasy.

## Enemy archetypes (the Amps)

| Enemy | Teaches | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Runner | basic pressure | anything |
| Sparkjack (shock-staff) | timing | Slip or Yank — trading hits loses |
| Breaker (tower shield) | positioning | Yank the shield or Spike from above |
| Jammer pod | priorities | Kill first: it disables reticle assist (Static's tech) |
| Drone [BUILT] | speed | Ram at 20 m/s |
| Brownout (boss) | everything | His darkness kills YOUR speed bonus — fight from light into dark |

Encounter grammar: rooftops and depot yards, 4–8 Amps, always with anchor
points overhead — every arena is also a traversal space.

# PART VI — PROGRESSION **[NEW]**

## Skill tree — three branches, 24 nodes

**LINEWORK (traversal):** Longer tether · faster reel · double-fire
(re-anchor mid-swing) · wall-run · updraft mastery **[BUILT: updrafts]** ·
Skyhook (brief anchor-in-air, T4 capstone).

**LIVE WIRE (combat):** Flurry ext. · Yank variants · Counter-loop ·
Spike AoE · speed-damage multipliers · Live Wire discharge (capstone).

**GRID SENSE (tech/detective):** Shard radar pings · drone route
prediction · jam resistance (counters Static) · beacon overcharge (co-op
buff auras) · evidence intuition (hub board hints) · Wren's Ear capstone
(hear enemy comms).

**Currency:** shards **[BUILT: 36]** + mission completion. Skill points are
STORY-VISIBLE: Didi physically installs tier hardware in hub scenes.

## Suit tiers (character upscaling, visible)

| Tier | Look | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| I | Dad's patched line-suit | start |
| II | Re-rigged, Wren's comms array | end Act 1 |
| III | Didi's anchor spool, plated | M11 (Noa scene — she reinforces it) |
| IV | The Linewalker mantle, cape solidified | Act 4 |

## Relationship scenes (the hub) **[NEW]**

Safehouse above Didi's shop between missions: evidence board, suit bench,
three conversation slots per act (Wren / Didi / Noa / later Cole). Short,
skippable, voiced-text scenes; choices color tone, not plot. This is where
the Marvel-style "Peter's life between swings" texture lives.

# PART VII — WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE BUILD

## Already true in the current build (no work)

Beacon campaign = Act 1 · drone hunt = Act 2 skeleton · shards = evidence
chain · districts named · co-op = Linewalkers · toasts = Wren/Kilowatt lines.

## Retrofit (small): re-voice existing toasts to Wren/Kilowatt speakers; add
speaker names to the toast UI; retitle objectives per mission.

## New systems by priority

1. **Hand-to-hand combat + human Amps** (Part V core loop) — biggest lift,
   biggest payoff.
2. **Hub safehouse + dialogue scenes** (Part VI) — cheap, huge story value.
3. **Skill tree + suit tiers** — economy exists (shards) already.
4. **Static's jamming mechanic** — HUD/audio degradation, very cheap, very scary.
5. **Brownout boss arena** (M15), then **M16 finale orchestration**.

## Scope honesty for the 20-day clock

Acts 1–2 retrofit + combat core + skill tree + hub = achievable.
Acts 3–4 are WRITTEN (this document is the asset) and ship as the
post-deadline roadmap — a game with a finished saga bible and two polished
acts beats four rushed ones.

# PART VIII — v1.1 ADDENDA

## Line Calls — the saving loop **[NEW]**

The saga is about holding the line for strangers; the game needs strangers to
hold it FOR. **Line Calls** are ambient emergencies Wren routes between
missions, spawning dynamically across districts:

| Call | Setup | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Stuck lift | Elevator dead between floors, blackout aftermath | Reach roof, Yank the brake release, lower on your line |
| Live wire down | Arcing cable near pedestrians **[G: peds built]** | Ground it: grapple-pull within a timer while civilians flee |
| Runaway | Brakeless car on a street **[G: traffic built]** | Chase at speed, attach, drag-anchor it to stop |
| Ledge call | Civilian frozen on a high ledge | No timer, no failure — walk, talk, clip them to your line. The QUIET one; different dialogue every time |
| Amp shakedown | Amps pressuring a shopkeeper | The fight loop in miniature |

Rules: never mandatory, always interruptible, small shard/relationship
rewards — but the FINALE remembers: districts where you answered calls send
visible help in M16 (crowds shelter Linewalkers, Amps defect faster there).
Saving people is the invisible difficulty slider of the last mission.

## Heat — the trouble system **[NEW]**

"Getting into trouble" as a mechanic, in three eras matching the acts:

- **Act 1 — POLICE HEAT:** Cole's unit responds to public vigilantism.
  Heat rises from property damage and fighting near cops; at max, cruiser
  pursuit + spotlight drones. Escape = break line-of-sight for 20s (pure
  traversal, no combat vs police — Ari's hard rule, and Cole notices).
- **Act 2–3 — GRID HEAT:** Kilowatt meters your movement; high heat swaps
  patrol lanes toward you, deploys Jammer pods, and prices your head to the
  Amps. Managed by destroying watch nodes.
- **Act 4:** no heat. The city knows. That absence IS the storytelling.

## Linewalker rigs — co-op roles **[NEW]**

Each player picks a rig spec (visual + one mechanical twist; all can swing):

| Rig | Twist | Fantasy |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchor** (default) | Balanced, the bible's Ari kit | The lineworker |
| **Splice** | Faster reel, shorter tether | The acrobat |
| **Ballast** | Slower air steer, heavier Spike + Yank | The wrecking ball |
| **Relay** | Weaker combat, aura: nearby allies gain reel speed + jam resist | The operator's favorite |

Specs are loadout, not lock-in; swap at the hub. Co-op comp becomes chatter:
"we need a Relay for M15."

## Sound identity **[NEW]**

- **Grapnel's motif:** a rising two-note interval (the "thwip-thunk" pitched)
  — the SFX literally play the leitmotif; music grows out of the foley.
- **Kilowatt's motif:** a 50/60Hz mains hum, harmonized. When he speaks, the
  room key-shifts to the grid frequency. Subliminal ownership.
- **Rule of silence:** M11 (Noa) and the Ledge Calls have NO music. The
  saga's loudest moments are unscored.
- Implementation: extends the procedural WebAudio kit **[BUILT]** — motifs
  are oscillator patterns, not recorded tracks; the whole score ships in code.

## Difficulty & accessibility **[NEW]**

Assist toggles (no "easy mode" stigma, itemized instead): aim assist
strength, swing auto-level, combat timing windows, motion-sensitivity
options (FOV kick off, camera smoothing up), photo-sensitive mode (no
strobing in Static missions), subtitle size/speaker tags. Difficulty
philosophy: enemies get smarter lanes, not fatter health bars.

## Director's rulings — LOCKED (v1.2)

1. **Ari is player-selected.** Body/voice chosen at Tier I suit-up; all
   dialogue stays address-neutral (already written that way). Implementation:
   HeroRig gains body presets + accent color choice; the story never
   contradicts any selection. Marketing face: the SUIT, not the person —
   which is itself thematic (anyone can hold the line).
2. **Kilowatt ends in custody.** As written — the wiring-diagram beat stays.
   Sequel hook remains the Harbor South radio call, NOT an escape.
3. **Title: THE KILOWATT SAGA.** GRAPNEL survives as the hero brand and
   splash-screen mark. Future campaigns are new sagas (the Harbor South call
   seeds "THE UNDERTOW SAGA"). Retrofit: title screens and README.
4. **Rating: darker edge.** Ari's no-kill code is ABSOLUTE — but the world
   is not. Villains kill on-screen: Brownout's substation wave has named
   casualties (M10 gains a memorial beat that fuels the Noa scene); Static's
   betrayal left a whistleblower dead before the saga began (retcon woven
   into M12's files); the Surge is explicitly lethal stakes. Ari's restraint
   now COSTS something, which is the point of a code.
